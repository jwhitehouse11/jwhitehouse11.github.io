I am on the 2026-2027 academic job market.

I am a Stanford Artificial Intelligence Laboratory (SAIL) postdoctoral fellow at Stanford University, where I am fortunate to work with Vasilis Syrgkanis, Ramesh Johari, and Emma Brunskill. I am also affiliated with the Stanford Causal Science Center (SC2) and am an external researcher with Airbnb.

My research is broadly focused on problems in statistics, machine learning, and applied probability. Much of my work has been focused on causal inference, in particular the reliable estimation of (heterogeneous) treatment effects and optimal decision making. Here are some questions I have been thinking about:

  • Data Fusion: How can a learner combine multiple imperfect data sources in order to identify and estimate a causal effect of interest?
  • Assessing the Value of Personalization: When should a learner deploy a complex, potentially opaque treatment policy over a simpler, explainable one?
  • Sequential Decision Making: How should a learner sequentially make decisions to maximize a reward, particularly in settings with a large number of treatments or rich heterogeneous treatment effects? Further, how should one adapt classical statistical machinery to be applicable on adaptively generated trajectories?
  • Using and Improving Black-Box ML Predictions: How should a learner improve the quality of black-box ML predictors of heterogeneous treatment effects? How can a learner use synthetic predictions to augment causal estimators?

While my research is focused on developing statistical procedures that can be used in practice, the development of said estimators often involves interacting with rich statistical theory. Some examples of theoretical topics I am interested in include empirical process theory, self-normalized martingale concentration, non-regular statistical inference, and dimension-agnostic concentration.

Before starting as a postdoc at Stanford, I received my PhD in computer science from Carnegie Mellon University. There, I was advised by Aaditya Ramdas and Steven Wu, and was supported by the NSF Graduate Research Fellowship Program. Prior to my PhD, I was an undergraduate at Columbia University in New York City, where I was an I.I. Rabi Scholar. There, I majored in mathematics and computer science.
